Maker

I turn racing experience into patented engineering.

02.01 — Maker

The more I raced, the more I wondered what else could be built on top of an RC car. Two consecutive years of Sunday-afternoon STEAM classes at school gave me plenty of inspiration.

02.02 — Maker

The open-source 3D-printed chassis we ran in HSRT was an early attempt — but writing the organizing committee’s official chassis specification from scratch still ran into plenty of problems.

02.03 — Maker

My first idea was to convert a 1/10 electric touring car — adding a flight controller, GPS, FPV video transmission and telemetry to turn it into an unmanned ground vehicle; then fitting a Jetson Nano, camera and LiDAR with the open-source DonkeyCar stack to make it a self-driving intelligent car.

02.04 — Maker

News of Meituan’s drone food-delivery service in Shenzhen sparked the next idea: combine idle RC cars with the strengths of drones, design a storage compartment, and develop a “Micro Flying Car with Storage Structure” for low-cost last-mile transport. The project went through four versions and has been granted three national patents.

02.05 — Maker

Through these projects I became convinced that RC-based research is the best STEM education there is — and the best way for a racer to improve. So I developed the “STEM Public-Welfare Course Kit for Adolescent Students Based on RC Cars”: the e-book RC Cars, Real Science: A STEM Journey for Teens, three sets of 3D-printable RC chassis, and a step-by-step club operation guide. It also served as my project paper for the CTB (China Thinks Big) competition — and laid the foundation for my later STEM Education Equity Initiative.

02.06 — Maker

After joining my school’s research group, I could finally pursue a long-held idea: a tool to help beginners set up an RC car quickly, reducing errors, wear and frustration. This became my S.-T. Yau High School Science Award (Physics) paper — I investigated the mechanism of thermal–quasi-static similarity failure under scale distortion, designed five controlled-variable experiments, corrected the governing equations, and developed the Fast Setup Decision Tree (FSDT).

02.07 — Maker

With that research experience, I began upgrading the micro flying car once again — systematically studying multimodal decision-making and structural optimization to turn it into low-cost, rapidly deployable mountain emergency-rescue equipment. The paper has been submitted to an EI-indexed journal.

02.08 — Maker

Setbacks are the norm in research, and problems persist to this day — but the recognition along the way keeps me moving forward.
