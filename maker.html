Maker

I turn racing experience into patented engineering.

02.01 — Maker

The more I raced, the more I wondered what else could be built on top of an RC car. Two years of Sunday-afternoon STEAM classes at school gave me plenty of inspiration.

02.02 — Maker

The open-source 3D-printed chassis we ran in HSRT was my earliest attempt. The motor, ESC, servo and transmitter were all off-the-shelf, but writing the organizing committee’s official chassis specification from scratch still ran into plenty of problems.

02.03 — Maker

For upgrades, my first idea was to convert a 1/10 electric touring car: add a flight controller, GPS, FPV video transmission and telemetry to turn it into a driveless car, then fit an NVIDIA Jetson Nano, camera and LiDAR with the open-source DonkeyCar stack to make it an intelligent car.

02.04 — Maker

News of Alef Aeronautics’ Model A first flight in the United States sparked the next idea: combine idle RC cars with the strengths of drones, design a storage compartment, and develop a Micro Flying Car with Storage Structure for low-cost last-mile transport. The project went through four versions and has been granted three national patents, and I already have a roadmap for the next upgrade.

02.05 — Maker

Through these projects I became convinced that RC-based research is the best STEM education and the best way for a racer to improve. So I developed the STEM Public Welfare Course Kit for Adolescent Students Based on RC Cars: the e-book RC Cars, Real Science: A STEM Journey for Teens, 3D-printable RC car kits, and a step-by-step club operation guide. It is the first RC-STEM public welfare course kit in China to integrate open-source 3D printing, AI and a competition system; its research paper won Highest Distinction (Top 5%) + Most Creative Solution at the CTB National Conference, and it laid the foundation for my STEM Education Equity Initiative. 02.06 — Maker

After joining my school’s research group, I could finally pursue a long-held idea: a tool to help beginners set up an RC car quickly, reducing errors, wear and frustration. This became my S.-T. Yau High School Science Award (Physics) paper. I investigated the mechanism of thermal–quasi-static similarity failure under scale distortion, designed five controlled-variable experiments, corrected the full set of governing equations, and developed the Fast Setup Decision Tree (FSDT).

02.07 — Maker

With that research experience, I began upgrading the micro flying car once again: systematically study multimodal decision-making and structural optimization to turn it into low-cost, rapidly deployable mountain emergency-rescue equipment, including a dual-mode energy-aware algorithm, foldable hardware and special-shaped blades. The paper has been submitted to an EI-indexed journal.

02.08 — Maker

Setbacks are the norm in research, and problems persist to this day, but the recognition along the way keeps me moving forward.
